Live $0.016/min selected

The system listens and writes the transcript down while the therapist and client are still talking. It's complete the instant the session ends.

  • Use it whensomething needs to happen during the session — live safety-word flagging, live captions, or any tool that has to react to what's said in the moment.
  • Why it costs moreGoogle has to hold a continuous connection open and process audio as it arrives, which is priced as a premium "streaming" service.
  • Data footprintno separate recording has to be made — audio is processed and discarded as it streams, so nothing extra is stored beforehand.
Dynamic Batch $0.003/min selected

The session is recorded, then submitted to Google's discounted "Dynamic Batch" queue — a specific, named pricing mode, not just "the non-live option." The discount is a deliberate trade: Google can take up to 24 hours to return it, in exchange for the lower price.

  • Use it whena same-day (or next-day) transcript is genuinely fine — end-of-week documentation, billing records, retrospective note-writing. Not for anything you or the client will want to see today.
  • Why it costs lessthis is Google's real, published "Dynamic Batch" rate (confirmed 4x cheaper than the standard $0.016/min) — it exists specifically to sell spare processing capacity in exchange for accepting a long, unguaranteed wait.
  • The trade-offup to 24 hours before the transcript exists, by Google's own design — not a technical limitation to engineer around, but the deliberate price of the discount. It cannot support anything that needs to happen live, or even same-hour.
  • Data footprintthis mode needs a recording of the session to exist, even briefly. Recording a session is a separate thing to get patient consent for, on top of transcribing it, and needs its own privacy sign-off — the lower price comes with more paperwork, not less.

In short: live costs more because you're paying for the transcript to exist the instant it's spoken. Dynamic Batch costs a quarter as much, but the trade isn't a short delay — it's Google's own up-to-24-hour window, plus an extra consent/privacy step for keeping a recording in the first place. There's no cheaper middle ground: a "chunk the audio to fake live" approach doesn't get the discount, because the discount is specifically for accepting the 24-hour wait, not for using the batch endpoint (see notes below).

Notes on these figures

  • Rates sourced from Google's official pricing pages, verified 7 Jul 2026. Both Chirp and the Gemini 2.5 series price identically regardless of region — europe-west4 (NL) costs the same as global, so residency is free here.
  • Transcription volume tiers ($0.016 → $0.010 → $0.008 → $0.004 per minute) are applied as graduated bands past 500k / 1M / 2M minutes a month, the standard model for usage-based cloud pricing — worth confirming against Google's actual billing behaviour if volume gets close to a threshold.
  • The $0.003/min Dynamic Batch rate is confirmed directly against the live Speech-to-Text pricing page (SKU 7700-6778-EF8E), not a placeholder — but it only applies when the request explicitly opts into Google's DYNAMIC_BATCHING processing strategy, which caps latency at up to 24 hours. There is no cheaper tier for "batch, but fast": sending short audio chunks to the batch endpoint without that flag bills at the same $0.016/min as live, and Google's own docs say batch recognition isn't designed for clips under 60 seconds in the first place — so chunking audio to emulate live transcription would not save any money.
  • Figures are model-inference and storage cost only: orchestration (e.g. n8n), logging and human-review time are not included.
  • The transcript storage total assumes a steady state: once the practice has been running longer than the chosen retention period, old files age out roughly as fast as new ones arrive, so total stored data plateaus at (monthly volume × retention) rather than growing forever. In the first few months — before that many months have actually elapsed — the real bill will be lower than this figure.
  • The 40 KB default transcript size is a rough estimate for a 45–60 minute conversation rendered as plain-text/markdown — measure an actual sample transcript and adjust the slider once one exists.
  • Voice agent scope is grounded in the practice's actual phone workflows (belafspraak scheduling, cancellations, first-line "spoed" triage before human handoff) rather than open-ended conversation — call volume itself isn't published anywhere, so treat calls/day as your own assumption to tune.
  • Telnyx undercuts Twilio on every telephony metric checked (inbound, outbound, and WebSocket media streaming) — it's the default carrier here. Number rental isn't counted since the practice would port its existing +31307984022 rather than rent a new one.
  • Chirp 3 HD (TTS) gets 1,000,000 free characters/month before the $30/1M-character rate applies — at low call volume this can fully absorb the bill, which is why the TTS line may show $0 with an "within free tier" tag.
  • Cloud Run hosting for the Pipecat orchestrator isn't in the total because it's effectively $0/month at this call volume (well inside the free tier) — but that only holds if the orchestrator's work happens synchronously within the request/WebSocket context. If it needs independent background threads (e.g. for turn-detection), it requires "always-on CPU" billing instead, which is materially more expensive and worth a design review before committing to Cloud Run at higher volume.
  • Document processing assumes Google's documented spec: a flat 258 tokens for a small image or one PDF page, regardless of resolution, at the same rate as text input (no multimodal surcharge). One live caveat: an open, unresolved Google issue reports Vertex AI (what this account runs on) sometimes returning up to 7× more tokens than that spec for the same image compared to the separate Gemini Developer API — confirmed for standalone images, not yet confirmed either way for PDF pages specifically. Worth testing against a real sample document with the CountTokens API before finalizing a budget on this line.
  • The € figure is a simple conversion at the rate entered above — edit it to the live rate for precision.
