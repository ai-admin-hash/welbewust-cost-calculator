Roles and Roadmap
Who does what in the client journey, from the day a referral letter arrives to the day the dossier is locked. Part 1 defines every role, internal and external; part 2 walks the nine phases and names each role's actions per phase; part 3 lists the documented detours; part 4 decodes the Dutch terms this document keeps. Compiled from the internal SOP sites.
1
The roles
The process runs on more than "admins, psychologists, patients". The SOPs distinguish four lanes; the colour of each lane is used throughout the roadmap in part 2.
Practice
Secretariat (Anna, Samed, Quincy…) — owns the administrative pipeline: registration, screening, scheduling, sending the treatment agreement, GP letters, invoices and insurance claims. Executes; the clinicians verify.
Management / board / FG (Massy and Maaike) — the owners. Maaike is also the privacy officer (FG) and the clinical escalation point for doubtful and crisis cases.
TP student / intern — student intern assigned to the Sport & Therapy administration (registration, Calendly, dashboard).
Clinicians
Coordinating therapist (RB) — always a GZ-psychologist or psychotherapist. Does the intake, makes the diagnosis, owns the treatment plan and every claim-critical form, signs referrals and locks the dossier at close-out. Every client must always have one.
Treating therapist (MB) — often a master's-level psychologist. Runs the sessions, writes the session notes, drafts letters and referrals, does the first close-out pass; consults the RB on every plan change.
Clinical psychologist (KP) — the specialist-care gate: reviews intakes for care-need types 5–7. Only an RB may consult the KP.
Client side
Client — an active participant, not a passive subject: fills in screenings and outcome questionnaires, co-decides the treatment plan (shared decision making), signs the treatment agreement, and carries an exercise goal through the whole track.
Family & close others — have their own involvement policy and confidentiality scripts; involvement is discussed at intake and can be arranged during treatment.
External
GP / referrer (incl. the practice nurse, POH-GGZ) — opens the journey with the referral letter, receives the start-of-care and closing letters, and takes declined clients back.
Health insurer — silently governs the roadmap: coverage, reimbursable diagnoses, session ceilings per care-need type, and payment of claims.
NUL30 Gym (contact: Ali) — sport partner: free trial class at intake, weekly supervised training during treatment.
External specialist care / psychiatrist — the onward-referral endpoint when scaling up inside WelBewust is not enough.
2
The roadmap — nine phases
Phases A–E build the paper foundation (referral → screening → intake → treatment agreement), F spends it, G–I prove and seal it. Almost every administrative rule exists because the insurance claim at the end only pays out if the chain was correct from day one.
A
Registration and referral letter
gate: valid referral letter · valid 9 months
Everything starts when a registration lands in the tracking sheet — but insured mental healthcare requires a referral, so this phase is entirely about getting a valid referral letter into the dossier. It is the foundation the eventual insurance claim stands on.
ClientRegisters on the website; chases their GP for a referral letter if needed.
GP / referrerWrites and sends the referral letter (ZorgDomein / KSYOS); receives a formal amendment request when criteria are missing.
SecretariatClaims the row, creates the dossier, checks the six referral criteria, runs the coverage check (COV), flags EHIC/AON.
Health insurerPassively decisive: the coverage check plus the care-possibilities table decide if a track is even possible.
B
Screening
gate: good fit for WelBewust
Is this person right for us? The screening sorts every registration into good fit (invite to intake), doubt (scripted telephone screening with a full risk inventory, then internal consult) or not a fit (back to the GP). Sport is arranged here too — exercise is baked into every WelBewust track.
SecretariatSends and assesses the screening; runs the scripted phone screening in doubtful cases; arranges sport and EHIC paperwork.
ClientFills in complaints, suicidal thoughts, help request, previous treatment, substance use, living situation, exercise.
Management / FGMaaike is the doubt/crisis consult; same-day escalation Anna → Maaike → GZ-psychologist on site → Arunya.
TP studentRuns the Sport & Therapy administration.
GPTakes "not a fit" clients back and is informed.
NUL30 GymFree trial class; onboards enrolled clients.
C
Scheduling the intake
only with a GZ-psychologist
A matching problem: on the call-back appointment the client is paired with the right GZ-psychologist (ADHD list, complex cases to experienced clinicians), booked as 60 + 30 minutes, and the quiet but crucial Fase 4a checklist prepares the dossier so the intaker walks in fully equipped.
ClientBooks the call-back, picks the slot, activates Embloom.
SecretariatMatches on the help request, checks the capacity sheet, books the intake, and completes Fase 4a: confirmation mail · ZPM form · e-health · outcome questionnaire set out · RB added.
Coordinating therapistVerifies every step ("secretariat executes, RB checks").
Health insurerSession budget per care-need type consulted live on the call.
D
Intake
ID + policy-number check mandatory · 60 + 30 min
The clinical heart of the front end. After nine preparation checks, the conversation opens with two mandatory administrative acts (ID and policy-number check) and then does the real work: complaints, diagnostic direction, and a treatment plan built together with the client. What is written here flows straight onto the treatment agreement.
Coordinating therapistPrepares, checks identity, writes the intake report with a SMART treatment plan + exercise goal, discusses options and the family-involvement policy (shared decision making).
ClientIdentifies, co-decides plan and goals (informed consent), brings the prepared exercise goal.
Family & close othersPolicy discussed; involvement agreements possible.
SecretariatFixes anything the intaker flags via the standard mails.
E
Post-intake processing
48 hours · agreement in 3 working days · signed BO = gate
The most deadline-dense phase: 11 administrative steps in fixed order, within 48 hours. Each form feeds the next, and one wrong date makes sessions unclaimable months later. The treatment agreement goes out within 3 working days — and without the signed agreement, treatment cannot start.
Coordinating therapistExecutes the 11 steps: intake form → care-need typing → number of sessions → diagnoses → DSM-5 → ZPM → register RB → treatment agreement → GP letter (start of care) → ROM score → supplementary data.
Clinical psychologistReviews specialist-care intakes (types 5–7) and co-reads the treatment plan.
SecretariatSends the agreement + reminders, files the signed return, sends the start-of-care GP letter, plans session 1 with a treating therapist.
ClientSigns the agreement; fills in the first SQ-48 in Embloom.
GPReceives the start-of-care letter.
Health insurerReimburses the intake regardless; caps specialist tracks (max 11 sessions where no long track is allowed).
F
Treatment
sessions: type × insurer · claims every 10th
The long middle, with two loops in parallel. Clinical: sessions of 45 + 15 minutes, evaluation every time, the RB pulled in for every plan change (which needs a new agreement). Financial: locked appointments flow automatically via Vecozo to the insurer every 10th of the month.
Treating therapistRuns sessions and session notes; session 1 re-verifies the diagnosis is reimbursable; consults the RB on changes, stagnation or scaling up.
Coordinating therapistOversight in the background; approves changes; sole access to the clinical psychologist.
SecretariatPlans 3 sessions ahead, processes no-shows and invoices (WeFact), runs the claims and resolves validation errors.
ClientAttends (48-hour cancellation policy), does e-health and sport, pays the deductible.
Family & close othersInvolved per the involvement policy.
Health insurerPays or rejects claims; bills the client's deductible directly.
NUL30 GymWeekly supervised training.
G
The second-to-last session
CQi non-anonymous
Short but deliberate: one session before the end, the measurement is armed — SQ-48 and the satisfaction survey in Embloom — so results exist before the final conversation and the last session can evaluate instead of promising to measure later.
Treating therapistSets out SQ-48 + CQi with invitation mail; asks a satisfied client for a review.
ClientFills in both before the last session; optionally writes the review.
H
The final session
consent for the closing GP letter
The therapeutic close: first and last questionnaire compared to make improvement visible, treatment and sport evaluated, relapse prevention where needed — and with the client's consent, the closing letter to the GP is written together.
Treating therapistEvaluates results, goals and sport; drafts the closing letter with the client.
ClientEvaluates, consents (or not) to the GP letter, keeps Embloom access.
GPNamed recipient of the upcoming closing letter.
I
Full dossier check
closed within 2 weeks · MB → RB → secretariat
The administrative close — and the SOP is blunt about ownership: the clinicians, not the secretariat. A three-party chain checks and locks everything, because the final claims only succeed if the whole chain was correct from day one.
Treating therapistROM and CQi into Careweb, end-of-care GP letter, activity and notes check, own end date; signals the RB and detaches.
Coordinating therapistChecks and locks the DSM-5 form, Zorgtraject ZPM, care-need typing and the intake form; submits "Einde traject"; detaches.
SecretariatFinal double-check, sends and locks the closing letter, archives. Anna collects reviews (anonymously on the website) and is alerted, with the RB, on any satisfaction score of 5 or lower.
GPReceives the closing letter: client discharged, course of treatment, advice.
Health insurerReceives the final claims.
3
Detours — off the linear path
Closure after intake (after D/E) — the RB concludes no reimbursable track is possible; GP informed.
Scaling up to specialist care (during F) — MB signals, RB agrees, KP reviews, new agreement; bounded by the insurer.
Group therapy (from C) — ADHD, generalised-anxiety and depression groups: max 6 participants, 12 sessions + follow-up.
ADHD / autism assessment (parallel track) — 11-step protocol; autism pilot run by psychologists in GZ training under supervision; only with insurers that reimburse it.
External referral to specialist care (out of F) — MB drafts, RB signs, secretariat sends.
Coordinating-therapist handover (any moment) — secretariat informs and re-links; dates must dovetail to the day.
No-show and cancellation (during F) — 48-hour policy; invoice via WeFact; the agenda must stay true.
Crisis / suicidality (any moment) — same day: Anna → Maaike → GZ-psychologist on site → Arunya.
