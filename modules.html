The honest headline

Patients in treatment never pay. Everyone else can.

Dutch law is unambiguous: anything a therapist assigns is part of insured care, already paid inside the consult the insurer reimburses. The money is in selling the platform to other practices, insurers and the public — exactly how Therapieland built its business.

Question 1 — verified against NZa, Zorginstituut, LVVP & VGZ policy

Can we charge our patients for modules?

The rule is simple once you see it: the line is not digital-vs-paper, it is insured care vs everything else. Whatever the therapist assigns or monitors is insured care — already funded through the consult tariff. There is no separate e-health price, and none is allowed.

SituationCharge the patient?Why
Module assigned by the psychologistNO — prohibitedPart of insured treatment, paid inside the consult. The regulator (NZa) explicitly bans any extra fee on top of insured care — even a "voluntary" one.
Questionnaire / ROM used in treatmentNO — prohibitedDiagnostic and monitoring work is treatment. Same rule.
Extra module a patient picks themselves during treatmentGREY — treat as noIf it relates to what we're treating, it counts as care. Only sellable if truly separate wellness content — needs legal + insurer sign-off first. Default: don't.
Self-help bought after treatment ends (or by non-patients)YESNo active treatment to attach to. Normal consumer product: 21% VAT, clear pricing, standard consumer rules.
Subscription while in active treatmentNO — avoidA recurring charge on someone we're treating reads as paying extra for insured care. Highest-risk model; not worth it.

What the patient does pay: only their normal insurance deductible (eigen risico). Mental healthcare has no legal patient contribution — and our insurer contracts enforce this. VGZ's 2026 purchasing policy states it as a hard contract condition:

"De zorgaanbieder hanteert alleen een eigen bijdrage … voor zorg waar de verzekerde vrijwillig gebruik van kan maken, en niet voor verzekerde zorg ten laste van de Zorgverzekeringswet."

Why this is good news, not bad: it means the modules' value inside our own practice shows up as efficiency and quality (blended care within the same consult tariffs, replacing the Embloom subscription — now dropped) — while the revenue comes from everyone outside our treatment room.

Question 2 — between psychologist and patient

How the modules work in the app

The loop is built and live in Aweris: the psychologist assigns, the patient works at home, the system scores, the psychologist reviews. Modules meet the patient at every phase of their journey:

ScreeningPsychoeducation"What is panic?" — sent right after the screening verdict, so waiting time becomes preparation time.
Before intakeQuestionnairesSQ-48 baseline (already automated) + intake forms — scored before the first conversation.
In treatmentExercisesThe psychologist assigns CBT/ACT/DBT exercises matching the treatment plan; patient completes them between sessions.
Between sessionsDiariesMood, sleep, worry check-ins — small, repeated, trend-charted.
ClosingRelapse prevention + CQiA closing module and the patient-experience questionnaire we must report anyway.

For the psychologist this is assign-and-review, not extra admin: pick from the library (filtered by therapy model), see completed work with scores and risk alerts on the patient profile, and string modules into ready-made programs ("Depression — CBT, 8 weeks") that unlock step by step. For the patient it's one calm place: their assigned modules, their own progress, in their language.

Question 3 — the part that decides trust

How we handle the results

Results are the most valuable and most sensitive part. The rule: a result belongs to the patient's care, never behind a paywall — and it must be handled as clinical data. Two different worlds, deliberately kept apart:

Inside treatment — clinical data

Scored, guarded, logged

  • Questionnaires are auto-scored with clinical thresholds (e.g. PHQ-9 severity bands, SQ-48 subscale profiles) and trend-charted over time.
  • Risk answers raise an immediate alert to the treating psychologist (e.g. the PHQ-9 suicide item) — a human responds, never software alone.
  • Results live in the patient's record, visible to patient and treating clinician; every access is logged (the NEN 7513 requirement in our compliance plan).
  • The patient can always see and export their own results as a PDF — free, always.
  • Aggregates feed the reporting we already owe: CQi and SQ-48 exports for insurers and national benchmarks — anonymised, never individual.

Rule of thumb: the moment a result flows to a therapist, it's clinical care — free to the patient, protected, logged.

Outside treatment — consumer product

Private by design

  • The public self-help workbook stores answers only on the user's own device — no account, no server, nothing sent to us.
  • It computes no scores and no diagnoses — reflections, not verdicts. That's deliberate: it keeps the product light and outside medical-device rules.
  • The user can export or print their own summary and choose to bring it to a therapist — their choice, their data.
  • If we later sell a richer results view, it must stay non-clinical (no severity labels, no alerts) — or be built as a certified medical device. We choose non-clinical.

This airtight privacy story — nothing leaves your device — is itself a selling point no big competitor offers.

Before we can sell

Five gates, all known and manageable

  1. Instrument licences. Our 149 exercises and 68 psychoeducation modules are own content — free to sell. But ~9 questionnaires carry licences that block commercial resale (4DSQ, SQ-48, HoNOS, the APA measures, and a few more). Plan: quarantine those from any paid product ("bring your own licence") and ship the ~26 verified-free instruments. Two licence e-mails (4DSQ, SQ-48) could unlock the most valuable ones.
  2. Clinical review & publish. Only 16 of 577 module files are published today — the rest wait for clinical sign-off (Anna + behandelaren, the in-app review flow exists). Zero code, pure review work.
  3. Multi-practice build. Aweris is single-clinic today. Selling to practice #2 needs tenant separation, onboarding and billing — roughly 3–6 weeks of build.
  4. Processor agreements. Selling to practices makes us their data processor — we need a standard verwerkersovereenkomst (template work, once).
  5. Insurer confirmations. Before any paid feature touches insured patients: written confirmation from each contracted insurer, per their own purchasing policies.

Bottom line

The library is real: 304 evidence-cited modules, 80% our own content, in a working platform. We cannot — and don't need to — charge our own patients: inside WelBewust the modules pay for themselves in efficiency and replaced subscriptions. The business is outside: licence the platform to other practices at €25–35 per practitioner, sell self-help to the public, and let insurers fund the rest through the routes that already exist. Patients get it free, protected and scored; everyone else pays.
