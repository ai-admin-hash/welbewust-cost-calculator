The module system · challenge + three proposals

How do we make money from the modules?

Massy asked for two things: define the challenge in detail, then three proposals to solve it — and a call to decide. Here they are: the problem stated honestly, three distinct routes to revenue, a side-by-side comparison, and a recommendation. Grounded in the legal and market research already done.

The challenge, defined

We built something valuable — and it earns €0.

We have 304 evidence-cited therapy modules (CBT / ACT / DBT), automatic ROM (SQ-48, CQi, PHQ-9…), and a working platform around them. Inside our own practice it saves ~€3.000/month. As an external product it currently makes nothing. Two walls explain why the obvious moves don't work:

Wall 1 — we can't charge our patients.Anything a therapist assigns is insured care, already paid inside the consult. The NZa forbids an extra patient fee — verified. So our own clients can never be the customer.
Wall 2 — modules alone are a commodity.Therapieland and Embloom sell libraries too. A pile of content isn't a moat; the value has to be in how it's delivered, to whom, and what it's bundled with.

So the challenge is: turn the module library from an internal cost-saver into external revenue — knowing we can never bill our own patients, and content by itself isn't defensible. Every proposal below is really an answer to one question: who pays, and what exactly are they buying — the platform, the experience, or the content? (Constraints to respect throughout: a handful of licensed questionnaires can't be resold; NEN 7510 is the entry ticket for selling to practices; and we're a small team.)

Proposal 1 — the platform play

Sell the whole platform to other practices

License Aweris — modules + ROM + screening + the clinician panel — to other GGZ practices, per clinician per month. You become a software company; WelBewust is customer #1 and the proof. This is the Therapieland model.

Who paysOther GGZ practices & institutions. Their patients stay free.
What they buyThe running platform + support — not just content.
Price€25–35 / clinician / mo (Therapieland: €35,75)
Revenue potentialHIGH — 10 practices ≈ €9k/mo; ceiling €50k+/mo
EffortMulti-tenant build (~3–6 wks) + sales + processor agreements + NEN 7510.
Main riskIt's a real company: sales, support, competing with incumbents.

Proposal 2 — the consumer play

Sell self-help directly to the public

Package the library as a consumer self-help product — for non-patients: former clients, people on a waitlist, the general public, or employers. You become a consumer brand. The on-device workbook is already built; this adds payment, accounts and reach.

Who paysConsumers directly — or an employer / insurer subsidising them.
What they buyThe self-guided experience (programs, tracking).
Price€19–39 per program or €5–10/mo · 21% BTW
Revenue potentialMEDIUM — volume-driven; also a lead funnel to the practice
EffortWorkbook ≈ built; add payment + accounts (~2–4 wks). Marketing-heavy.
Main riskCustomer-acquisition cost; must stay strictly non-patient.

Proposal 3 — the wholesale play

License the content to other platforms

Sell the module content itself — 304 items, NL + EN, evidence-cited — to other e-health platforms or EPDs that have distribution but thin content. You become a content supplier, not a platform. Lowest build: the content already exists.

Who paysOther software vendors / EPDs (Careweb-type players).
What they buyA licence to our content library, in their product.
PricePer-module licence or a 10–30% revenue share
Revenue potentialMEDIUM — a few €k–€20k/mo per partner; deal-dependent
EffortLowest build (content ready) — but the licensed-instrument red-list must be cleared first.
Main riskCommoditised; partner owns the customer; not defensible.

My recommendation for the call

Lead with Proposal 1 — but they stack

Proposal 1 (platform → practices) is the engine. It has the highest ceiling, we own the customer, WelBewust is a live proof-point, and it's the path already scoped in the roadmap. It's the one worth committing real focus to.

The other two are cheap add-ons that feed #1, not competitors to it:

  • #2 (self-help) is a low-cost funnel + brand builder — the same workbook doubles as the sales demo for #1. Ship it small, let it generate leads.
  • #3 (content licensing) is opportunistic — take a deal if a platform comes knocking, but don't lead with it: it turns us into a replaceable supplier.
  • All three respect the wall: none of them ever charges our own patients-in-treatment. That's non-negotiable and already verified.

Bottom line for the call

The challenge is real: a valuable library that legally can't be sold to our own patients and isn't unique on its own. The answer is to sell it to someone else — and the strongest "someone else" is other practices, buying the whole platform (Proposal 1), with self-help as the funnel and content-licensing as opportunistic upside. Recommendation: pick Proposal 1 as the focus, run Proposal 2 alongside as the demo/funnel. Let's decide on the call.
